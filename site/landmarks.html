The Riemann hypothesis for elliptic curves

Over a finite field k with q elements, a smooth Weierstrass cubic is a smooth finite curve of genus one, read inside its own function field: its two charts are integrally closed, and its closed points and their residue degrees are those of a smooth curve at the Weierstrass charts. The Weil conjectures for curves over finite fields

For a smooth, projective, geometrically irreducible curve of genus g over a field with q elements, held by two charts: the effective-divisor series is the Euler product over the closed points and has the extension counts N_m as its logarithmic derivative; Riemann–Roch makes it P(T)/((1 − T)(1 − qT)) with P an integer polynomial of degree 2g, P(0) = 1, the functional equation q^g pₙ = qⁿ p₂g₋ₙ, and a divisor of degree one; Bombieri's bound from one-point pole spaces and a Frobenius tensor map, carried to every extension through constant twists and good fibres, bounds every reciprocal root of P by √q, which is the Riemann hypothesis for curves and the Hasse–Weil bound |N_m − qᵐ − 1| ≤ 2g · q^(m/2); the same for a smooth projective curve cut out by homogeneous equations over any finite subfield k of any algebraically closed field M, with no hypothesis but M and the curve's own. Such a curve is carried along M into the algebraic closure of M(x), where its two-chart presentation lives: its genus, its point counts and the size of k are read over k and kept, and its geometric conditions (a prime ideal, a point, the Jacobian rank) cross by elementarity. The line X₂ = 0 of the projective plane is a proved instance; the Weierstrass cubic is a stated one. The complex numbers enter only to split P.
