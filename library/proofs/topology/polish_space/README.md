# polish_space — complete separable topologies and countable products on normalized function carriers

A Polish topology has a compatible complete metric and a countable dense subset. The empty space is allowed. Products are product_topology's `indexed_product_topology`: formed on indexed_functions, with a prescribed inactive value, the topology generated by restricted coordinate cylinders.

`indexed_discrete_topology` is the product of discrete factors whose carrier may vary with the coordinate; the based-dual and Čech cochain topologies are its instances. `FiniteCoordinateContinuous` applies to maps between subspaces of such products: each active output coordinate must depend on a fixed finite set of input coordinates on the source carrier, and the map must send that carrier into the target carrier. It requires no countability or algebra; nor does `FiniteSupportDense`, the density of the finitely supported functions. For countably many countable discrete factors, `CountableProductPolish` supplies the ambient Polish topology, and polish_group.DiscreteProductGroupPolish the group structure. `LusinSouslin` speaks of Borel sets: a continuous map of Polish spaces carries a Borel set on which it is injective to a Borel set, and polish_group's image theorem stands on it. A standard Borel space is a measurable space whose σ-algebra is the Borel σ-algebra of some Polish topology on its carrier; countable discrete sets, countable products and the line are standard Borel, and conditional distributions with values in one exist (probability/conditional_distribution). The remaining open statements are marked as cards below.

`CountableProductPolish`, in `products.go`, takes as its metric the
countable product distance `countable_product_metric(J, d, h)` along a
sequence h running through the active indices: it is a metric
(metric_space.CountableProductMetric), complete when the factors are
(complete_metric.CountableProductComplete), and its topology is exactly
the product topology (metric_space.CountableProductMetricTopology).
Separability is `product_topology.IndexedProductSeparable`.

## Polish models and applications

General product continuity requires continuous finite-coordinate functions, not only finite dependence. Finitely supported points are dense for arbitrary product topologies with admissible base points: that is product_topology's `ProductFiniteSupportDense`, which `FiniteSupportDense` reads at discrete factors. `CountableProductPolish` holds for arbitrary Polish factors, not only discrete ones.
