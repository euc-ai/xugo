# lefschetz — characteristic-zero truth and eventual prime-characteristic truth

Large-characteristic stability reads a sentence through its common
quantifier-free equivalent over the algebraically closed fields on two
types. An atom of a closed quantifier-free sentence is a closed integer
polynomial, which vanishes exactly where its constant coefficient c does:
in characteristic zero when c is zero, and in a prime characteristic p
when p divides c, which past |c| again means that c is zero. A nonzero
constant thus vanishes in only finitely many characteristics, and a
quantifier-free sentence has finitely many atoms, so its truth takes one
value in characteristic zero and in every prime characteristic past a
bound. A fixed host supporting an algebraic closure of every prime field
makes eventual truth nonvacuous. The complex field supplies the
characteristic-zero instance.

A closed sentence true in some algebraically closed field of every prime
characteristic is true in every algebraically closed field
(`PrimeFamilyTransfer`): in a prime characteristic by same-characteristic
transfer from that prime's model, and in characteristic zero by stability,
from the model of a prime past the bound. Elementarity (`ByElementarity`)
cites it when its source is such a family of models, one for each prime.

| Member | Statement |
|---|---|
| `AtomStability` | A closed polynomial atom takes one truth value in every field of characteristic zero and every field of prime characteristic past some bound. |
| `QuantifierFreeStability` | Closed quantifier-free truth stabilizes from characteristic zero to sufficiently large prime characteristic. |
| `LargeCharacteristicStability` | Closed ring-formula truth stabilizes between characteristic-zero and large-prime-characteristic algebraically closed fields. |
| `PrimeClosureLocallyFinite` | Every prime characteristic has a locally finite algebraically closed model in the fixed host. |
| `ComplexModel` | The complex field is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. |
| `LefschetzPrinciple` | A ring sentence holds over the complex numbers exactly when it holds in all sufficiently large prime characteristics. |
| `PrimeFamilyTransfer` | A closed sentence true in an algebraically closed field of every prime characteristic is true in every algebraically closed field. |
