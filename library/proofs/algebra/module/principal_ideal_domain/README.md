# principal_ideal_domain — finite generation and freeness over principal ideal domains

Over a principal ideal domain a linear functional φ splits a submodule along one scalar d: φ carries it onto the ideal d·R, and for d ≠ 0 a lift b of d writes each vector, in one way, as a vector φ kills plus a multiple of b (`LinearFunctionalSplit`); the last coordinate of a finite free module is one such functional (`CoordinateProjectionSplit`). A submodule of a module with a finite basis E is free on at most card(E) vectors (`SubmoduleFree`): by induction over the finite parts of E, it splits along the basis-coordinate functionals through `LinearFunctionalSplit`, one at a time. A finitely generated torsion-free module is free (`FiniteTorsionFreeFree`): a finite generating set has an independent part whose span holds a nonzero multiple c • g of every generator, scaling by c is one-to-one, its image is a submodule of the free span of that part, and its basis pulls back along the scaling (`linear_independence.BasisPullback`). Inside a field, a finitely generated S-submodule is free of finite rank over a principal ideal domain R ⊆ S over which S is finitely generated (`FinitelyGeneratedFree`): finite generation passes down the tower R ⊆ S (`FiniteModuleTower`, generators multiplying across it by `fractional_ideal.SubringTowerSpan`), and a submodule of a field is torsion-free.

Over a principal ideal domain a submodule of a finitely generated module is finitely generated (`FinitelyGeneratedSubmodule`). The module is the image of the free module on a finite generating set, whose delta functions are a finite basis (`free_module.DeltaAtBasis`, `free_module.FreeLiftOntoSpanning`); the preimage of the submodule is a submodule of that free module (`module_homomorphism.HomPreimageSubmodule`), free on finitely many vectors by `SubmoduleFree`, and their images generate the submodule. The integrality criteria that follow ask no principal ideal domain. An element x of a field L is integral over a subring S exactly when its polynomial values over S form a finitely generated S-module (`IntegralFiniteModuleCriterion`): a monic relation of degree d leaves the powers of x below d to span the polynomial values, and conversely, when finitely many generators are values of polynomials of degree at most n, $x^{n+1}$ is the value of a polynomial supported below n + 1, and the monomial of degree n + 1 minus that polynomial is a monic relation. An element x of L that multiplies a finitely generated S-submodule containing one into itself is integral over S (`StableModuleIntegral`): a finite generating set spans that submodule in L and one is a nonzero member of it, so the determinant trick for a nonzero S-module finitely spanned in L applies (`integral_element.FinitelySpannedIntegral`), the trick that also closes the elements integral over S under sum and product (`integral_element.IntegralAddNegMul`). So every element of a subring of L finite over S is integral over S (`FiniteSubringIntegral`), and an element integral over a subring B finite over S is integral over S (`IntegralTowerOverPrincipal`).

The values k[t] of the polynomials over a subfield k at an element t of a field form a principal ideal domain (`PolynomialImagePrincipalIdealDomain`), the instance the curve chapters read. When t is transcendental over k, a nonzero prime ideal of k[t] is generated by p(t) for a monic irreducible p over k (`NonzeroPrimeMonicIrreducible`): the generator the PID gives is scaled monic, and a factorization of it would put a factor of smaller degree in the prime. A finite free k[t]-submodule M of the field has M/dM finite-dimensional over k for every nonzero d ∈ k[t] (`PolynomialModuleQuotientFinite`), so M/V is finite-dimensional for every submodule V that spans M over k(t) (`FullSubmoduleQuotientFinite`). A ring homomorphism from a principal ideal domain onto a ring leaves every ideal of the target principal (`SurjectiveImageIdealsPrincipal`): the preimage of the ideal is the multiples of some d, and the image of d generates the ideal. A principal ideal domain S inside a field is integrally closed in its field of fractions K (`PrincipalIdealDomainIntegrallyClosed`): for x in K integral over S, one denominator in S clears the finitely many powers spanning S[x], so the conductor of S[x] into S is a nonzero ideal g·S, and g·x, lying in it, is g·r with r in S; so k[t] is integrally closed in k(t) (`PolynomialRingIntegrallyClosed`).

Finite generation, torsion freeness and common-denominator localization remain distinct hypotheses; finite generation of an algebra is never substituted for finite module generation. Over a subring S of a field whose fraction field is K, a finitely generated S-submodule inside the K-span of another is carried into it by one nonzero d of S (`FiniteModuleInclusionAfterLocalization`), and two finitely generated S-submodules with the same K-span have the same saturation at one such d (`FiniteModulesSameLocalization`), each saturation lying in the other since d carries each submodule into the other (`ModuleSaturationMono`).
