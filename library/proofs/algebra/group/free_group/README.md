# free_group — the free group over an alphabet

The free group over a type A is the group generated by the elements of
A subject to no relation beyond the group laws. The chapter builds it
the way every presentation does: as words over the alphabet, read up
to the cancellation of a letter against its inverse.

A LETTER is a generator with a sign, a pair `(a, b)` of an element of A
and a boolean: `(a, ⊤)` is the generator a, `(a, ⊥)` its inverse, and
`letter_inverse` flips the sign. A WORD is a list of letters, its
inverse (`word_inverse`) the word read backwards with every letter
inverted. Two words are EQUIVALENT (`word_equiv`) when one is obtained
from the other by inserting and deleting adjacent pairs `x, x⁻¹`. The
relation is defined by four rules — reflexivity, symmetry,
transitivity, and the one cancellation — so it is an equivalence by
its first three rules and is generated by the fourth; the chapter
proves it is a congruence for concatenation and for inversion.

The free group `free_group(A)` is the quotient of the words by this
equivalence, a type minted by the quotient door with
`free_group_of_word` — written ⁅w⁆, the class of the word w — as its
class map. The product of two classes is the class of the
concatenation, the inverse of a class the class of the inverse word,
the identity the class of the empty word, and a generator a is the
class of the one-letter word `(a, ⊤)`. Each operation is a lift: the
congruence law is its respectfulness, and the quotient's own facts —
every element is the class of a word, equal classes are equivalent
words — are what every law over the free group reduces to.

The chapter's theorem is that this is a group: `group_on(mk_group(free_mul, free_one, free_inv, 𝕌))`, an instance of the group chapter's structure, so
every law of `algebra/group/group` reads at it. Associativity is the
associativity of concatenation, the identity law the empty word's, and
the inverse law is the one piece of mathematics — a word's inverse
concatenated with the word cancels letter by letter to the empty word.

## The definitions

| entry | statement |
|---|---|
| `letterInverseDef` | `letter_inverse(x) = (fst(x), ¬snd(x))` |
| `wordInverseDef` | `word_inverse(w) = list_reverse(list_map(letter_inverse, w))` |
| `wordEquivRules` | the four rules of `word_equiv`: reflexive, symmetric, transitive, and `word_equiv(u ++ (x :: x⁻¹ :: v), u ++ v)` |
| `WordEquivEquivalence` | `equivalence(word_equiv)` |
| `FreeGroupDef` | `free_group(A)` — the quotient of `list(prod(A, bool))` by `word_equiv`; `⁅w⁆` its class map |
| `FreeOneDef` | `free_one = ⁅nil⁆` |

## The congruences and the lifts

Word equivalence is a congruence for both operations on words, and
that is exactly what lets them descend to the classes. Each one-sided
concatenation law is a rule induction along the equivalence: the three
equivalence rules carry the property for free, and the cancellation is
the same cancellation at a re-associated word. The inverse laws are
computations with the definitions and the list chapter's laws; the
inverse congruence is the one rule induction among them, its
cancellation case the one computation (the inverse of `x, x⁻¹, back` is
the inverse of `back` followed by `x, x⁻¹`).

| member | statement |
|---|---|
| `AppendCongruenceLeft` | `word_equiv(u, v) ⇒ word_equiv(u ++ w, v ++ w)` — appending a word after an equivalent pair keeps it equivalent |
| `AppendCongruenceRight` | `word_equiv(v, w) ⇒ word_equiv(u ++ v, u ++ w)` — appending a word before an equivalent pair keeps it equivalent |
| `AppendCongruence` | `word_equiv(u₁, u₂) ∧ word_equiv(v₁, v₂) ⇒ word_equiv(u₁ ++ v₁, u₂ ++ v₂)` — the equivalence is a congruence for concatenation, the respectfulness the lift of the product cites |
| `LetterInverseInverse` | `letter_inverse(letter_inverse(x)) = x` — inverting a letter twice gives the letter back |
| `InverseAppend` | `word_inverse(u ++ v) = word_inverse(v) ++ word_inverse(u)` — the inverse of a concatenation is the concatenation of the inverses, in the other order |
| `InverseInverse` | `word_inverse(word_inverse(w)) = w` — the inverse of an inverse is the word itself |
| `InverseCongruence` | `word_equiv(u, v) ⇒ word_equiv(word_inverse(u), word_inverse(v))` — equivalent words have equivalent inverses, the respectfulness the lift of the inverse cites |
| `FreeMulDef` | `⁅u⁆ * ⁅v⁆ = ⁅u ++ v⁆` — the product of two classes is the class of the concatenation (a lift; `AppendCongruence` its respectfulness) |
| `FreeInvDef` | `free_inv(⁅u⁆) = ⁅word_inverse(u)⁆` — the inverse of a class is the class of the inverse word (a lift; `InverseCongruence` its respectfulness) |

## The group

Each axiom is read at representatives — every element is the class of
a word, the operations compute at classes, and class equality is word
equivalence — so associativity and the left identity are the
concatenation laws of the list chapter, and the left inverse is the
chapter's one piece of group mathematics: a word's inverse followed by
the word reduces to the empty word, by induction on the word, the
adjacent pairs deleted from the middle outward.

| member | statement |
|---|---|
| `InverseCancel` | `word_equiv(word_inverse(w) ++ w, nil)` — the inverse of a word followed by the word reduces to the empty word |
| `Group` | `group_on(mk_group(free_mul, free_one, free_inv, 𝕌))` — the free group over A is a group: each axiom read at representatives, associativity and the left identity the concatenation laws, the left inverse `InverseCancel` as a class equation |

The chapter form is the constitution's (`library/chapter_constitution.md`);
the one line of mechanics: the type and its seven facts are the
quotient door's, and each lifted operation names its congruence law as
its `Respects`.
