# euclidean_domain — integral domains with a division algorithm, whose ideals are principal

A Euclidean domain is an integral domain with a measure δ into the natural
numbers such that dividing by a nonzero element always leaves a remainder
that is zero or of smaller measure. Its ideals are principal.

## Members

| Member | Statement |
|---|---|
| `EuclideanDomain` | `euclidean_domain`: ring_ops(A) → (A → num) → bool |
| `IntegralDomain` | A Euclidean domain is an integral domain |
| `Division` | Dividing a by a nonzero b leaves a quotient and a remainder that is zero or of smaller measure than b |

## The ideals are principal

An ideal holding a nonzero member is generated by a nonzero member of
least measure, which well-ordering supplies; an ideal holding only zero
is generated by zero.

| Member | Statement |
|---|---|
| `MinimalMeasureGenerates` | A nonzero member of an ideal whose measure is least among the ideal's nonzero members generates it |
| `PrincipalIdealDomain` | Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain |
