# ring_away — a subring with one element inverted inside a ring where it is a unit, and the integral closure with that element inverted

A ring with one element t inverted, S[1/t], can be built as classes of
fractions s / tⁿ (algebra/ring/ring_localization). When S is a subring of
a ring R in which t already has an inverse — a subring of a field above
all — this chapter builds it in place: `ring_away(R, S, t)` is the set of
members of R that a power of t brings into S, a subring of R holding S
and the inverse of t, and the inclusion of S into it is the localization
of S at the powers of t. A ring with an element inverted is then a
subset of the ambient ring, and two of them are compared by inclusion
and equality. Inside a field, inverting a unit adds nothing, inverting x
and then y is inverting x · y, and S[1/t] is the least subring holding S
and the inverse of t. The overlap of the two charts of a curve inside its
function field (scheme/algebraic_curve) is a ring of this kind.

Inverting an element is carried through what a subring of a field
holds: an ideal of S[1/t] is generated by its meet with S; a ring map
from S to a field k inside it that fixes k and does not vanish at t
extends to S[1/t]; a spanning set and a basis of a ring C over S stay
one of C[1/t] over S[1/t]; a principal ideal domain stays one; and
inverting an element commutes with the integral closure in a field.

## A subring with one element inverted, inside a ring

Where t already has an inverse u in a ring R, a subring S with t
inverted need not be built as fractions: it is the set of members of R
that a power of t brings into S, `ring_away(R, S, t)`, and this
inclusion is the localization of S at the powers of t.

| member | statement |
|---|---|
| `RingAwayDef` | `ring_away(R, S, t)`: the members r of R with tⁿ · r ∈ S for some n |
| `RingAwayMember` | `r ∈ ring_away(R, S, t) ⇔ r ∈ R.carrier ∧ (∃n. tⁿ · r ∈ S)` |
| `AwayCoverMember` | an element carried into `S` by a power of each member of a set generating the unit ideal of `S` lies in `S` |
| `RingAwaySubring` | `S` a subring of `R` containing `t` ⇒ `S ⊆ ring_away(R, S, t)`, a subring of `R` holding the inverse of `t` when `R` has one |
| `RingAwayIdeal` | `I` an ideal of the subring `S` holding `u` ⇒ `ring_away(R, I, u)` is an ideal of `ring_away(R, S, u)` containing `I`, in a field `R` |
| `RingAwayProducts` | `S ⊆ R`, `t ∈ R`, `t · u = one` ⇒ `ring_away(R, S, t)` is the products `a · uⁿ` with `a ∈ S` |
| `RingAwayInSubring` | `S` a subring of `R` holding `t`, with `C ⊆ S` and an inverse `u ∈ S` of `t` ⇒ `ring_away` of `C` at `t` computed in `S` is `ring_away(R, C, t)` |
| `RingAwayIsLocalization` | `S` a subring of `R` holding `t`, with `u ∈ R.carrier` and `t · u = one` ⇒ the inclusion of `S` into `ring_away(R, S, t)` is the localization at the powers of `t` |
| `RingAwaySpanOfContraction` | `R.carrier = ring_away(R, S, d)` with `d · e = one` ⇒ every ideal `J` of `R` is the ideal its contraction `S ∩ J` generates |
| `InclusionIsLocalizationAway` | `R.carrier = ring_away(R, S, d)` with `d · e = one` ⇒ the inclusion of `S` into `R` is the localization at the powers of `d` |
| `HomomorphismExtendsAway` | a ring map `S → k` fixing `k` and not vanishing at `d` extends to a ring map `S[1/d] → k` fixing `k`, whose kernel is the ideal the first kernel generates and meets `S` in it |
| `KernelSpanAwayFieldResidues` | the same hypotheses ⇒ `ideal_span(R, ker φ)` is the ideal of a k-rational point of `R` (`rational_ideal(R, k, R.carrier, ·)`) and meets `S` in `ker φ` |
| `IndependentAway` | independence over S ⊆ L survives inverting a nonzero t ∈ S (one direction) |
| `SpanningAway` | inside L, a set E spanning a ring C over a subring S spans C[1/t] over S[1/t], for a nonzero t ∈ S |
| `BasisAway` | inside L, a basis of a ring C over a subring S is a basis of C[1/t] over S[1/t], for a nonzero t ∈ S |
| `CommonPower` | inside L, when a power of s ∈ S brings each member of a finite E ⊆ L into an S-submodule N, every power from one exponent on brings all of E into N |
| `SpanOverAwayIsSaturation` | inside a field with s ≠ 0, an S-submodule X spans over S[1/s] exactly its saturation at s |
| `SaturationIsAway` | the saturation of B at a in the regular module of R is B with a inverted |
| `AwayOfUnit` | `u · v = one` with `u, v` in a subring `S` of a field ⇒ `ring_away(S, u) = S` |
| `RingAwayTwice` | `x, y` in a subring `S` of a field ⇒ `ring_away(ring_away(S, x), y) = ring_away(S, x · y)` |

| `AwayPrincipalIdealDomain` | A principal ideal domain inside a field stays one with a nonzero member inverted |

## The integral closure with one element inverted

Inverting a member t of R commutes with the integral closure in a subfield K (`IntegralClosureAway`). Both inclusions are one move on a monic equation: a monic equation of x over R[1/t] has its coefficients brought into R by one power tᴺ (`PolynomialCommonPower`, `CommonPower`'s form for the coefficients of a polynomial), and a root scaled by an element that clears the coefficients is integral over the smaller ring, so tᴺ·x is integral over R; conversely $x = t^{-N} \cdot (t^N \cdot x)$ is integral over R[1/t].

| member | statement |
|---|---|
| `PolynomialCommonPower` | when each coefficient of a polynomial is brought into S by some power of t ∈ S, one power brings them all |
| `IntegralClosureAway` | inverting t commutes with the integral closure in a field: the closure of R[1/t] is the closure of R with t inverted |
